What the Pier-Ferry EIR does not anticipate

Subject: Berkeley Water Transportation Pier Ferry Project, Final EIR (SCH 2025040995), Council certification hearing 2026-11-17
Prepared: 2026-11-05, from the Berkeley Bay Flood Explorer repository (SOURCE_NOTES P10–P14, W20, C30; E22)
Status: a working memo. Nothing here is a flood forecast. The explorer compares water levels; it does not predict them.
At the Pier-Ferry project's own design water levels, the Final EIR does not look at whether anyone can drive to the pier. In the explorer's still-water fill, every street route from I-80 to the pier crosses Bay water at both levels. The EIR's answer to BCDC's question on ferry-terminal access is about ferry service, and it does not mention the roads.

1. What the EIR plans for

  • Design still water, 2075. The project's design still water level is 11.9 ft in the EIR's "Berkeley MLLW" datum with the Intermediate sea level rise scenario. After BCDC's Engineering Criteria Review Board, the governing condition became the Intermediate-High scenario, at 13.3 ft Berkeley MLLW (Final EIR §2.2.23, p. 2-51; COWI response of 2026-04-10). sourced
  • Converted to NAVD88 with the EIR's own table. 11.9 becomes 11.3 ft NAVD88 and 13.3 becomes 12.7 ft NAVD88 (Final EIR Table 3.8-1, PDF p. 137). derived These are still water levels; the EIR treats waves separately.
  • The flood finding. Draft EIR Impact HYD-4 (pp. 3.8-39 to 3.8-40) finds construction and operation in the 100-year floodplain and tsunami zone less than significant, because of "climate-adaptive design elevations, watertight storage, battery safety systems" and best management practices. The finding concerns pollutant release. sourced

2. What the EIR does not anticipate

a. Access to the pier by road. BCDC's comment A3-48 asked the City to "discuss the potential for interruptions to ferry terminal access in the analysis of sea level rise" (Letter A3, Final EIR PDF p. 119). The response (PDF pp. 138–139) discusses ferry operations: service "would likely be suspended" in a 500-year storm, and public access to the pier "may be temporarily restricted". It does not mention the roads between I-80 and the pier. A text search of the Draft and Final EIR for University Ave, Marina Blvd or Spinnaker Way near "flood", "inundat" or "sea level" found nothing. We did not read both documents in full. sourced

b. Its own environmental framing. The Final EIR's sea level rise topical response (§2.2.23, pp. 2-51 to 2-53) says the comments concern "the impact of the environment on the project, rather than the impact of the project on the environment". It states that CEQA does not require that analysis, and it answers the comments "nonetheless". Whether the roads are part of "the project" is a question the EIR does not address. sourced

c. How long the water stays. The response says peak water levels in a 500-year storm "are expected to persist for a relatively short duration, typically less than four hours", so "any interruption in service would be temporary". The response cites no source for the duration, and the explorer does not model it.

d. Flood elevations quoted without a datum. The Final EIR's response to A3-49 (PDF p. 140) says "the BFE for Zone VE ranges from approximately 12 to 13 feet and the BFE for adjacent Zone AE areas is approximately 10 feet", citing FEMA's Map Service Center. No datum is stated. The FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer, read 2026-11-05, gives Zone VE, BFE 13 ft NAVD88, at the pier's base, and Zone AE, BFE 10 ft NAVD88, behind it, so the EIR's figures appear to be in NAVD88 (derived by matching). sourced derived

e. A conflict in the datum. The EIR's "Berkeley MLLW" is not NOAA's MLLW at Berkeley. Its own Table 3.8-1 says NAVD88 is "approximately 0.6 feet below Berkeley MLLW", but its table puts NAVD88 0.6 ft above it. NOAA's Berkeley station 9414816 gives MLLW +0.13 ft NAVD88, which puts Berkeley MLLW 0.73 ft below NOAA's MLLW at that station. The two sources agree on MHHW, MHW and MSL. sourced (both sources); derived (the 0.73 ft difference). The 11.3 and 12.7 ft NAVD88 values above use the EIR's own table.

4. What this does not establish

  • Not a flood forecast. The 11.3 and 12.7 ft NAVD88 levels are 2075 design conditions, not predictions of a storm.
  • Still water only. Waves on the open Marina shore add height. FEMA's transect Base Flood Elevations along Berkeley's shore, which include wave runup, are 12 to 16 ft NAVD88 (FIS Vol. 2, Table 10). Whether FEMA's still water figures include setup is open (SOURCE_NOTES C28 (b)).
  • Bare-earth terrain. The fill runs on lidar ground elevations, which remove structures such as overpasses and bridges. Whether a road would close depends on its profile and the water's depth and duration, which the model does not establish.
  • The street layer. Segment geometry comes from OpenStreetMap-derived data in the explorer's repository. Whether the mapped roads match the streets as built has not been checked against the City's street centerlines.
  • Scope of the search. The "not mentioned" finding in section 2a rests on a text search of the two EIR documents, not a full reading.

5. Questions the EIR could answer

  1. Does the City's analysis of the pier's access include the roads between I-80 and the pier, at 11.3 and 12.7 ft NAVD88 (and the EIR's Berkeley MLLW equivalents)?
  2. What is the source for the "less than four hours" duration in the response to A3-47?
  3. Which datum is the EIR's "Berkeley MLLW" referenced to, and is it NOAA's MLLW at Berkeley station 9414816? Table 3.8-1 and its text disagree.
  4. Which datum are the FEMA BFEs of 12 to 13 ft (VE) and 10 ft (AE) in the response to A3-49 referenced to? The FEMA map gives NAVD88.
